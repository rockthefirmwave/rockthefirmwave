Introduction#

Why I’m Here#

After over two decades in software development, mostly in C++ and C#, with a background stretching into HPC, signal processing, and even Fortran and assembly, I’ve decided to step into something new—embedded systems development. 

Not entirely new: Since school, I’ve had a good grasp of how computers work—things like memory, interrupts, and basic CPU architecture. But my day-to-day work stayed mostly in higher-level software. Now I’m finally diving into the world of real embedded systems: where you touch the hardware, build from the ground up, and deal with constraints that just don’t exist in regular software. And yeah, you can solder it out!

What I’m Doing#

I’m committing publicly to a journey: to learn, build, and share. The format will be simple:

  • I’ll move in small steps, from the very beginning, from bare-metal, to… maybe even taking full control of my foot massager? Well, let’s start with the LED blinking.
  • I’ll document what I’ve learned and what I’ve struggled with.
  • I’ll publish both successes and failures, including dead ends and tough challenges.
  • I’ll occasionally reflect on broader, related topics.

Why Public?#

This is a contract—not just with myself, but with anyone who finds this blog. By writing, I teach. By teaching, I learn better. By learning publicly, I stay accountable.

I believe in the approach of: learn it, explain it, master it.

And I believe that embedded systems are too important, too interesting, and too challenging to tackle alone.

What’s Next#

Next up: choosing a platform and setting up a working dev environment.

Feel free to follow along, ask questions, and challenge my decisions. This isn’t a polished tutorial series. It’s a hands-on journal of my learning process.

See you in the next post.
